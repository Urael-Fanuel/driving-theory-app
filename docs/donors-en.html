The problem nobody talks about

A theory exam in a language they can't read

When Ethiopian immigrants arrived in Israel, many of them never had the opportunity to learn to read and write — not in Hebrew, and sometimes not even in Amharic, their mother tongue. This isn't a matter of ability or willingness. It's simply the result of life circumstances that never afforded them that most basic opportunity.

For people in this situation, a theory exam built entirely around reading text and marking answers isn't a small technical hurdle — it's an impassable wall. Not because they don't understand driving. Because the exam tests literacy, not road safety.

The real cost goes far beyond a license not granted. Research on immigrant populations consistently shows that the ability to drive and hold a license is directly linked to higher employment rates, more work hours, and faster integration into society. Without a license, there's no employment independence — only constant dependence on others, and one more obstacle on the road to real inclusion.

A solution that already exists and works

An app with two learning engines — not one

I built a free app that teaches the entire driving theory curriculum in Amharic — traffic signs, road rules, and a full exam under real conditions (30 questions, timed, exactly like the real test). The content is based on the international Vienna Convention (used by 70+ countries) together with material from the Israeli and Ethiopian Ministries of Transportation — so the learning stays relevant whether the exam is in Israel, Ethiopia, or anywhere else.

🔊 Engine A — Voice

Can't read at all? No problem. Every question, every explanation, every sign — read aloud. Answers are given by speaking too. Zero reading required.

📖 Engine B — Text

For those who read Amharic — full written explanations, detailed answer breakdowns, and detailed progress tracking by topic.

The app is completely free, requires no registration or personal details, and works offline for most features — because the population it's built to serve is largely the population that can least afford additional obstacles.

Why this matters — beyond the individual

When one person gets a license, the whole society benefits

👤 Personal independence and dignity

A driver's license isn't just a piece of paper — it's the ability to get to work, take a child to the doctor, and stop depending on rides from others. It's the feeling of being in control of your life.

🏠 Impact on the family

A parent who gets licensed and finds a better job changes the economic trajectory of the whole family — and shows their children that obstacles can be overcome.

🌉 Bridging a social gap

Literacy is not a measure of safe driving ability — but it does determine who gets a license and who doesn't. This app corrects that distortion: it gives everyone an equal opportunity, regardless of their reading ability.

🛣️ Road safety for everyone

A driver who truly understood the material — not one who just memorized answers someone read aloud to them — is a safer driver. That's good for them, and good for everyone who shares the road with them.

Where things stand today

A living project, in progress, with no institutional backing

The app is built and active — including over 1,200 audio files and dozens of instructional videos in Amharic — and is currently in the approval process for an official launch on Google Play. But it's being built by one person, with no company or organization funding it.

This is the point where a donation makes a real difference, not a symbolic one: continued development — adding content, servers and storage for the audio and video files, and ongoing maintenance — currently depends entirely on self-funding. Your contribution doesn't "add to" an existing budget — it effectively is the budget. Without it, progress will slow down significantly; with it, the app can reach thousands more people, much faster.
